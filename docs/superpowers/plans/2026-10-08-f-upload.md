# F10 · Streaming without hitches on a weak GPU — plan (f-upload, 9 Oct 2026)

Branch f-upload, based on v042 (a00fd82: F8 fade-in and upload queue, F-Far ring 1, t-sky). Every edit to files other
fronts touch (renderer.js, world.js, horizon.js, farring.js, game.js, the language tables) is additive and kept in its
own methods; the merge notes are at the end.

## The complaint

The owner, verbatim: „irgendwie hängt das mit der Welt-Generierung usw." His PC: a Ryzen with 16 threads, 32 GB RAM, the
integrated AMD Vega (device 0x1638) sharing the system memory. Measured in his browser: GPU-bound at ~20 fps on Low at
720p, JS 5–6 ms a frame. A GPU-bound frame has ~44 ms of CPU slack, so a main-thread spike is absorbed; what lengthens a
frame is GPU-side work — and the GPU-side work that comes in bursts while the world streams is the uploads.

## What goes to the GPU today (v042) and why it hitches

| upload | path | budget |
|---|---|---|
| section meshes (opaque, cutout, trans, vxFar) | world.js uploadQ → renderer.uploadSection | 1.5–3 ms a frame (preset `upload`), **measured on the CPU side only**: `bufferSubData` returns after a memcpy into Chrome's transfer buffer, the copy into the shared-memory GPU happens later in the GPU process — the scheduler's `msPerByte` falls to the memcpy rate (~3e-7 ms/B) and lets several MB through in one frame |
| an oversized section | the same | waits four frames, then goes up **whole** in one frame |
| horizon tiles on Very Low, Low, Balanced (ring 1 off) | horizon.js `put` in the worker's answer | **none** — every answer goes up the moment it arrives; a flight brings them in bursts |
| far forms (High, Ultra) | farring.js → uploadQ | the section budget |
| mini-voxel detail (layer 3) | renderer.vxResidency | **none** — up to 6 meshes a frame, whatever their size |
| texture arrays | boot (behind the loading screen) and a look switch (game.js reloadTextures) | none (a one-time switch in the settings) |

## Design

### 1 · Measured upload cost (src/shared/stream.js `UploadScheduler`, renderer.js)
- **Calibration at world start** (renderer.calibrateUploads, called by the World constructor): a 1 MB buffer,
  `gl.finish()`, then `bufferSubData` 1 MB + `gl.finish()` twice, the faster one counts → `calibrate(ms, bytes)`.
  `finish` waits for the GPU process and the GPU, so the shared-memory path is in the number.
- **Running measurement through a fence**: at the end of every frame that uploaded (and every 4th quiet frame while the
  world streams, for the base line) `gl.fenceSync`; polled at the next frames with `clientWaitSync(sync, 0, 0)` — timeout
  0, never blocking; at most four fences pending. The time from the fence to its observed signal is the GPU's lag for
  that frame. The scheduler fits lag = a + b · bytes over the recent frames (exponentially weighted least squares): the
  slope b is the GPU's extra milliseconds per uploaded byte, the intercept a the frame's own work. (The lag is quantized
  by the frame — a fence is seen at the next poll — but the fit averages over frames whose upload volume differs, so the
  slope is unbiased; a single frame never moves it.) The GPU estimate follows the slope (eased, held within ½ … 8× of
  the calibration).
- **The estimate in use**: msPerByte = max(the CPU-side estimate, the GPU estimate). The CPU side keeps the old rule
  (rises at once, falls slowly): a machine whose JS-side copy is slow is slow.
- **Bytes from milliseconds**: the frame's byte budget = the frame's ms ÷ msPerByte; the frame's ms = the preset's
  `upload` × the governor's streaming rung × the adaptive scale × the frame factor, minus the debt of a forced frame.
- **Frame factor**: the preset's ms are for a frame at the 30 fps target; a machine whose usual frame (the median of the
  last 64) is longer gets proportionally more (max(1, median ÷ 33.3)) — the same share of its frame. (The owner's 50 ms
  frame: 1.5× — Low 3 ms a frame, 6 % of the frame. It also keeps the scheduler honest under the slow software renderer
  of the tests: a 1.3 s frame there is the same frame, slower.)
- **Adaptive scale** (AIMD on the frame time): a frame over max(target, 1.25 × median) while uploads were made in the
  last three frames halves the scale (never below ¼); a frame under 85 % of that adds 0.05 (back to 1). An overrun with
  no recent upload (a garbage collection, a menu) is not the uploads' doing and changes nothing.
- **Debt**: an item that has to go whole (the player's own edit, an atomic item bigger than the whole budget after four
  frames) may overspend one frame; the overspend is paid back from the next frames' budgets (a token bucket), so a burst
  is never followed by another.

### 2 · Smaller packets (renderer.js staging, world.js)
- A finished section mesh becomes a parted item: `renderer.newStage(opaque, cutout, trans, vx, vxFar, props)`;
  `stagePart(st, maxBytes)` writes up to maxBytes of the layers in order (whole vertices; a layer's range is taken from
  the arena on its first packet; a big layer is split across frames with `bufferSubData(…, srcOffset, length)`);
  `commitStage` swaps the staged ranges in when the last byte is up (props anchors and the mini-voxel RAM copy with them);
  `dropStage` gives them back. Until the commit the section draws its old mesh (or nothing, for a new column) — no flicker,
  no half section. A new column is shown (fade-in) only when every section is committed (checkShown, as before).
- A newer mesh of the same section, an unloaded column, a world torn down: the waiting item is aborted (its staged
  ranges released) at the next drain (`alive()`), or by `clear()`.
- The player's own edit (urgent remesh) goes first and whole, in the same frame.
- The mini-voxel detail (vxResidency) is queued as parted items too (no more six uploads a frame outside any budget).

### 3 · Priority by view
- Near lane order: tier 0 = in the view (the column's centre within the horizontal half field of view plus its own
  width) or within 24 blocks of the camera (the ground under the player), tier 1 = beside, tier 2 = behind; nearest first
  within a tier. `world.viewDir` and `world.viewCos` (the cosine of the horizontal half FOV, from the FOV setting and the
  canvas) are set every frame without allocation; the queue re-sorts every frame (an in-place insertion sort: the order of
  the last frame is nearly right), so a turn of the camera re-sorts at once.
- An item already in progress continues first; the player's edit before everything.

### 4 · One budget for everything that goes to the GPU per frame
- Two lanes in the one scheduler: near (sections, mini-voxel detail) and far (horizon tiles, far forms). While both wait,
  the far lane gets 30 % of the frame's bytes (a flight keeps its horizon), and each lane gets what the other leaves.
- Horizon tiles go through the queue on every preset now (before: only while ring 1 was on), in the horizon's own order
  (coarse and near first: the request priority); the forms likewise. Stale ones (the camera moved on, the tile was
  evicted) are dropped without spending bytes.
- Texture arrays (a Klassisch/Nahtlos switch: 1842 layers × 32 px × 6 mip levels × 2 arrays ≈ 20 MB, until now in one
  frame): renderer.queueTextures fills NEW arrays a slice of layers at a time (texSubImage3D with srcOffset) within the
  frame's bytes, ahead of the columns, and swaps them in when complete — the old look draws until then. A newer set aborts
  it; dropped for any other reason (its world went away) it is finished at once, so a look is never lost.

### 5 · Hitch meter
- The scheduler counts hitches: frames longer than twice the usual frame (2 × the median of the last 64, at least
  2 × 16.7 ms) in the last 10 s. `renderer.stats.hitches`, `uploadMs` (the uploads' CPU ms this frame), `uploadKB`,
  `upBudgetKB`, `upMBs` (the measured rate); the F3 line shows them (German first: g.f3.stream).

### As built (differences from the first sketch)
- The queue keeps one record shape per item (made once at push), so its loops stay monomorphic whatever mix of sections,
  detail, tiles, forms and textures waits; the distance is computed once per item; the lanes' extra passes only run when a
  far item waits. A near item may carry its own priority (the textures: -1, ahead of every column).
- The fence fit's memory is ~200 frames (λ 0.995) and it eases the estimate by 2 % per sample: over 20 seeds of a fake GPU
  (16.7 ms poll grid, ±6 ms noise) it lands within 0.86–1.20× of the true cost after 400 frames, and on a 50 ms grid (the
  owner's frame) the median is right but single runs scatter 0.5–1.7× — which is why the calibration (instant) and the
  frame feedback (fast) carry the first seconds and the fit only refines.
- Only the player's own edits (setBlock 4 | SB_EDIT) go whole over the budget; the simulation's urgent remeshes (fluids,
  fire, crops: 4 alone) stay in it (they are near the player, so they sort first anyway).

### Keep
- The world.js callers' API: `uploadQ.push({key, col, x, z, bytes, run})`, `size`, `prune`, `drain`, `drainUploads`,
  `stats.uploaded/uploadMs/uploadQueue` (tests read them); atomic items still work (the far-ring test's stub queue).
- `uploadBudget()` and the mesh-job starts (scheduleMeshes: four jobs per ms of the preset's budget) unchanged:
  test/frame_cost.js keeps its EXPECT 47f25156.
- No per-frame allocation: the queue sorts and compacts in place, the scheduler's result object, clock and scratch
  arrays are reused (the old drain made an object per item and two arrays per frame); `world.viewDir` is reused.
  (A fence is a GL object per upload frame.)
- Very Low: a settled world makes no upload, no fence (base-line fences only within 120 frames of the last upload),
  no extra GL call; while streaming the drain is cheaper than before.

## Measuring
SwiftShader renders on the CPU (a Low frame ~1 s at 1280 × 720): an upload's memcpy there is noise, so the flight runs
both builds against the same **model of a shared-memory upload path**, installed in the page before the game loads:
every byte handed to the GPU (bufferData/bufferSubData/texImage/texSubImage with data) queues GPU copy time; the frame's
sync (the one-pixel read of far.mjs) waits for it; `finish` waits for it; a fence signals only when the copies before it
are done. Rate: 15 ms per MB on the owner's frame (a 3 MB burst — what the v042 budget lets through when the queue is
deep — then costs one 50 ms frame: the smallest rate at which his hitches can come from the uploads), stretched by the
measured ratio of the software frame to his frame (Low 50 ms, Balanced 70 ms assumed) so the copy keeps its share of the
frame. The CPU-side timing of the old scheduler cannot see this time; the new one sees it through finish and the fences
— exactly the situation on his machine.
- The same 60 s flight (seed 1, noon, frozen weather, 1280 × 720) on Low and on Balanced, before (v042's dist, kept in
  .tmp_tests/before_dist) and after, in one browser; frames over 2 × the median are hitches; upload bytes and the model's
  GPU ms per frame; at the end both builds settle and are photographed from the same spot: the same columns shown, the
  pictures alike (test/shots/stream_end_<preset>_<build>.png).
- Very Low and High: frame ms in a fixed settled scene, both builds (no streaming: must be equal).

## Gaps
- The upload-path model's rate (15 ms per MB on the owner's frame) is an assumption: his real rate is unknown. The ⚙
  line (F3) shows the measured MB/s and the budget on his machine; a screenshot of it while flying would settle it.
- The harness's flight outruns the loader (a software frame takes seconds): the measured uploads are the tiles and the
  terrain that arrived, not a full ring of columns.
- Run 2 predates three commits (8813735 textures, 292c548, 2b41be4); those are covered by node tests only.
- Arena creation (a new 32 MB arena) is not budgeted (rare: a few per session).
- The shadow map's re-render after near uploads is not rate-limited by this queue.
- The fence fit is noisy on a 50 ms poll grid (single runs 0.5–1.7× of the true cost; the median is right).
- A test that settles with ticks only (rendering off) gets the per-frame byte budget too and no fence samples: on a slow
  measured upload path (the model on) the queue then takes more ticks to empty than v042's. Tests that wait for "no
  dirty, no pending" sections do not wait for the upload queue (neither did they before); `world.uploadQ.size === 0`
  (a getter) is the condition to add where a shot must show the newest meshes.
