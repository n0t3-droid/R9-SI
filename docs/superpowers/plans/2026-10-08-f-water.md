## Tiers (`waterTier`, from the preset; a switch in `PRESETS`, passed through `applySettings` → `renderer.setQuality`)

| preset | tier | planar reflection | caustics | light shafts under water | surface waves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Very Low (potato) | 0 | off: sky-LUT reflection only | off | off | Gerstner normal (3 octaves, ALU only) |
| Low | 1 | 1/4 res, near chunks | on | off | Gerstner |
| Balanced | 1 | 1/4 res, near chunks | on | off | Gerstner |
| High | 2 | 1/2 res, near chunks + horizon | on | on | Gerstner + sparkle |
| Ultra | 2 | 1/2 res, near chunks + horizon | on | on | Gerstner + sparkle |

Every tier-dependent branch is a uniform (`u_waterTier`), so Very Low runs the same program as before minus work.

## Order of work (TDD: a failing test first, small commits)
1. **Wave module** `src/shared/water_wave.js` (`VR.WaterWave`): three Gerstner octaves, wind-aligned, the height
   function, the normal (GPU-Gems form with the steepness term), the GLSL text generated from the same table so the
   constants cannot drift. Node test `test/water_wave.js`: height bounded by the amplitude sum, normals unit length and
   pointing up, determinism, the GLSL text carries the table, the body-size amplitude curve is monotone.
2. **Wind direction** in `weather.js` (a slow, seeded direction with gusts; `E.windDir` in `env.js`). Node test in
   `test/water_wave.js` for the direction bounds and determinism.
3. **Surface shader** (`TRANS_FS`, water branch): the Gerstner normal replaces the 6 texture fetches of `waveNormal`
   (Very Low gets cheaper), amplitude by depth below the surface (shoaling: shallow edges flatten, deep water rolls;
   the body-size proxy, because a per-vertex body size would need a vertex-format change), GGX-type sun sparkle,
   depth-fade alpha at shore edges so they never cut.
4. **Planar reflection** (`renderer.js`): mirror about the water plane found by a ray scan under the screen centre;
   oblique near plane (Lengyel) clips what is under the water; reflected terrain sections culled with the mirrored
   frustum; reflection FBO at 1/4 (tier 1) or 1/2 (tier 2) of the frame; sky pass with the mirrored inverse matrix
   and `u_skyOff` (the mirror camera offset) so the reflected sun and sky are exact. Tier 0 skips all of it.
   Node test: the oblique matrix keeps points above the plane and clips points below it.
5. **Caustics** (tier ≥ 1): projected onto the floor seen through shallow water (the floor point is the surface point
   pushed along the refracted ray by the depth); own warped-sine caustic function.
6. **Underwater** (composite pass): light shafts (tier 2, a uniform branch in the composite), the surface-crossing
   distortion (a short ripple wave in the composite after the camera passes the surface, driven by the CPU),
   bubbles from the player's head (particles), a swimming bob of the eye with the same wave function.
7. **Biome colour**: the mesher's per-column water tint becomes an absorption colour (clear lagoon, murky swamp,
   deep navy sea) in the existing absorption term.
8. **Rivers**: the mesher's flow direction (`rot` bits, already there for kind 6) drives the surface drift and a
   flow-aligned wave set; waterfall side faces get a downward streak; mist particles at the foot of falling water.
9. **Rain rings**: a hashed grid of expanding rings in the surface normal while it rains (tier ≥ 1).
10. Browser test `test/water.mjs`: a lake shore (noon, sunset, night), underwater, a river and a waterfall, on Very
    Low and High; screenshots to `test/shots/water_*.png`; the frame cost table written into this plan.

## How it is measured
- Fixed scene for the cost numbers: the Heartland plaza at noon, seed 1, 1280×720 (the Measuring rule), plus the
  lake shore scene for the water itself. Both on Very Low and on High, with the water effects off (the commit before)
  and on.
- `renderer.stats` (draws, triangles, `ms`) and per-pass `performance.now()` around the reflection pass; the GPU timer
  where `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` exists; in SwiftShader the CPU numbers are the ones to compare.
- Fill-cost model for the reflection: pixels × passes (1/4 res = 1/16 of the fill of a full frame, 1/2 res = 1/4).
