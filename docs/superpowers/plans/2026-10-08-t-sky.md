# t-sky: the trailer's sky — light blue at the horizon, dark space at the top, day on the land

Branch `t-sky` (base v041 + v042 wave, `6fae587`). The owner's words (8 Oct): "den Himmel wie in den Trailern … die
Kombination zu hellblau, und dann oben dunkel, und dann Tag unten bei uns. Das soll richtig ausstechen und extrem exakt
wie im Trailer sein." By day the trailer's sky is bright white-cyan at the horizon, light blue, then medium blue, and the
zenith is deep indigo-navy space with stars and the pink-blue nebula showing through the daylight; the ringed giant and
the blue-green planet stand in it; white cumulus at mid-height; the land below in full warm daylight.

Method, measured, not felt:
1. `test/sky_ref.js` (node) measures the frames: the sky in clear rectangles per frame (listed in the test, checked on
   overlays), 8 bands from the frame's horizon line (band 0) to the top (band 7), the trimmed mean colour per band, its
   CIELAB colour; the stars, local contrast and nebula of the top bands. It writes `docs/trailer/sky_ref.json`: the
   per-frame curves and the targets (the median of each Lab component over the day frames, over the dusk frames).
2. `test/trailer_skycurve.mjs` (browser) frames three places at four times on High (and two on Very Low), takes each
   picture twice (as it is, and with the sky painted magenta by the renderer's probe: the sky mask), samples the same
   bands from the land's horizon line to the top, and asserts the game's curve within ΔE 12 (CIE76) of the target per
   band, the space visible at the top by day, the horizon band bright and clean, a dark top with stars at night, and the
   Very Low sky pass not slower than the baseline's. (`test/trailer_sky.mjs` already exists — t-clouds' four shots — so
   the new browser test has its own name.)
3. The sky in `src/main/env.js` (`VR.Sky`: three stops per time of day and their heights) and `src/main/shaders.js`
   (`skyColor`: the three-stop gradient, the space factor), tuned offline first: the real `SKY_FS` rendered with Mesa
   (surfaceless EGL) through the composite's tone path, the bands measured with the test's own functions, the stops fitted
   through an inverse of the composite (scripts in the session scratchpad: `tsky/uni.js`, `render.py`, `curve.mjs`,
   `fit2.js`).
4. Before, after, proof: three browser runs at most.

## The ordered differences (largest first)

1. **The day sky's colour and gradient.** The game's day is lavender-violet from the horizon to the top (L* 53–75); the
   trailer's day is white-cyan at the horizon (L* 75), light blue (68), medium blue (44) and deep navy at the top (23):
   ΔE 25–51 in every band, every place, noon and 15:00.
2. **No dark top by day.** The top band is L* 53–72 against 23: the "oben dunkel" of the owner is missing.
3. **No space showing through the day.** Local contrast at the top 0.5–0.9 against 4.3; no stars by day (0–0.2 per 1000
   pixels against 2.6); the nebula only as a pink smear.
4. **The dusk palette.** The game's 19:00 is too dark and too purple in the lower half (L* 43–59 at the horizon against
   76, hue magenta-red against the frames' lavender), the top too light (L* 41 against 30): ΔE 12–36.
5. **The sun's glow by day** spreads lilac-white over a third of the sky at 15:00 (the trailer's day sky is clean).
6. **The far haze** is t-look's dark violet veil at every hour; by day the trailer's far hills fade into the white-cyan
   horizon.

## The plan (what changed, where)

All in the sky's own code; no generator, mesher, light or block change (gen_same, mesh_same, light_same untouched).

- `src/main/env.js` — `VR.Sky`: three stops (horizon, middle, zenith; linear HDR) for day, dusk and night, the stops'
  heights `[h1, h2a, h2b]` and the sun's halo; the palette by the sun's height (day above ~11°, dusk from ~2° above to
  ~6° below — TRAILER.md §2.1's long golden hour —, night below ~−16°); `space [from, to, day]` and `nebDay`; the far
  haze by day the horizon's colour toward the middle (`fog`), at dusk and night t-look's veil; the warm sunset glow
  halved by day; `on: 0` puts t-look's two colours back through the same stops (an A/B switch). E carries `skyMid`,
  `skyStops`, `skySpace`.
- `src/main/shaders.js` — `skyColor`: `mix(horizon, middle, smoothstep(0, h1, h))` then `mix(…, zenith,
  smoothstep(h2a, h2b, h))` instead of `pow(h, 0.42)`; the horizon's haze band and the sunset band as squarings; the
  small blue tint term folded into the stops; `cosmic` = night + day × `smoothstep(space)` (no golden term, no
  darkening mix: the zenith stop is space already); the nebula's day brightness `u_skySpace.w`; wisps of the nebula off
  its band high in the sky (no new noise: the band's own octaves); the stars at 0.8 of the space by day; no cirrus over
  the space; the sun's halo from `u_skyStops.w`. `hazeColor` follows the lower stops with one smoothstep and products
  instead of four pows. The reflections' env terms (terrain, furniture, water) take the middle stop for their upper
  colour (the navy zenith would turn every polished top face black).
- `src/main/renderer.js` — `common()` passes `u_skyMid`, `u_skyStops`, `u_skySpace` (three lines, additive).
- No change to skybodies.js or clouds.js: the planets stand in the new sky as they are; the cumulus stays at y 206–215.

The stops were fitted offline before any browser run: the real `SKY_FS` rendered with Mesa (surfaceless EGL, GLES 3)
through the composite's tone path at the test's cameras (`tsky/uni.js`, `render.py`, `curve.mjs`: the same bands and
mask as the test), a JS port of the gradient and the composite fitted by Nelder–Mead jointly over the views (canyon and
city, noon and 15:00 for the day; canyon and city at 19:00 for the dusk), the port's targets corrected by the residual
the nebula, stars and planets add (Mesa − port per band, Lab), three rounds. Offline result (Mesa): every band of
every view within ΔE 9.7 (day: canyon noon worst 6.2, canyon 15:00 8.5, city noon 5.9; dusk: canyon 9.7, city 5.3).
