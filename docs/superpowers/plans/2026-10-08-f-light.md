## Status of this continuation (the look fix)

The first F1 (commits 8a010d9, 93db054) was judged worse than v0.41 (the pictures in .tmp_tests/f1_vergleich.jpg):
washed out, pale, the sky bodies gone, fog and clouds a white haze. This continuation does four things:

1. **The base is now v0.41.** F1 was branched (205d00c) before t-clouds landed in v0.41 (the gas giant, the moons,
   the nebula veil, the cirrus, skybodies.js). The old comparison put the F1 sky against a v0.41 sky it did not have.
   `git merge v041` (commit 41c7271) brings the bodies back; the conflicts (build.js, game.js, renderer.js,
   shaders.js) are resolved there and listed in the commit message.
2. **The daylight model's chromaticity is refit (our own coefficients, the same Perez form).** Measured before the
   change, with `.tmp_tests/atmo_numbers.js` (noon, sun 66° from the zenith, T 2.3):

   | direction | F1 before (lin rgb) | sRGB | v0.41 palette (lin, noon) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | zenith | 0.18 0.31 0.60 | 118 150 204 | 0.09 0.20 0.68 (80 124 224) |
   | horizon, away from the sun | 0.96 0.86 0.87 | 251 238 240 (white) | 0.47 0.62 0.94 (186 207 249) |
   | horizon, sun side | 3.2 2.3 1.7 | white (glare band) | — |

   The published chroma fit whitens the horizon (x 0.32 there against 0.24 overhead): the A and E terms of the x and y
   fits. The refit keeps the Perez luminance shape (the horizon/zenith luminance ratio ≈ 2.9, as v0.41's) and the
   sun-side C and D terms, drops the A and E chroma terms, and sets the zenith chroma to a deep blue (x 0.215, y 0.205)
   with the horizon paler through the A term. Turbidity whitens the zenith chroma toward the white point; the zenith
   warms as the sun nears the horizon (dawn and dusk keep their orange). The aureole (C of the luminance) is damped to
   40 %. Scale 0.06: noon zenith sRGB (89, 126, 202) against v0.41's (80, 124, 224). The shader and the table are
   unchanged in form (the same packed parameters), so Very Low and the other tiers stay the same code.
3. **Exposure and grade.** The GPU exposure is kept within ±0.5 stops around 1 (0.707…1.414), eased up in 2 s and
   down in 3 s; its key is calibrated so that a clear noon scene wants exposure 1 (a bright day is not bleached). The
   time-of-day lift is zero by day: it is warm only while the sun is within ~8° of the horizon, blue only at night.
4. **Aerial perspective** keeps per-channel transmittance (blue leaves first) toward the sky colour, now a saturated
   one, not toward white. The sky pass stays after the land and objects (depth-culled), on Very Low through the
   table (ATMO_LUT) and LITE. t-clouds' u_cirrus and u_skyProbe stay in the sky pass; u_skyOff (F7, v0.42) keeps working.

Proof (what the tests check): test/atmos.js (node) — the sky's colours at noon, dusk and dawn, turbidity, the table,
the exposure easing; test/light.mjs (browser, noon, the fixed scene) — the sky bodies' pixel contrast against v0.41's
reference value, the mean luminance of the clear scene within 10 % of v0.41's, and a saturation floor. The reference
values are measured on v0.41 with the same code (.tmp_tests/v041build is a `git archive v041` build).

## Order (each step: failing test first, small commit)

   test/light.mjs measures the fixed scene. Baseline numbers are taken on the unchanged v041 code.

3. Sky shader: analytic base + LUT path, sun disc with limb darkening, moon phases, stars fade at dawn; sky pass
   moved after the terrain (depth-culled).

6. Shadows: PCF 3×3 (balanced), rotated poisson (high), slope-scaled bias, cloud shadows.

8. Near cascade on high/ultra (24 blocks), if the budget allows.
9. Screenshots (test/shots/light_*.png) at 06:00, 12:00, 18:30, 23:00 outdoors and inside a lit house, Very Low and
   High; looked at, and iterated.

## How it is measured

Fixed scene: default world, seed 1, the Heartland plaza near the spawn, 12:00 (ticks 6000), frozen time, 1280×720,
Very Low and High, averaged over 8 frames after the chunks settled. Per pass (ms, `gl.finish()`-bounded, i.e. the
real SwiftShader work, relative costs only): sky, shadow, terrain, horizon, objects, copy, clouds, trans, precip and
particles, hand, tsr, post. Also draws, tris, sections, shadow draws and the number of full-screen passes.

Look numbers (the six scenes of atmosphere.mjs, seed 424242, Balanced, noon, the same view): mean luminance, mean
saturation and highlight clipping of the sky band (top third) and the land band (bottom third), the GPU exposure, and
the body contrast (the ringed giant's disc against the ring around it, the view aimed at it).
